# Engineering PowerPoint Presentation Generation Requirements

David's full spec for the major presentation deck (and, where applicable, the Diagrams
& Tables Export deck), given verbatim on 2026-11-28. This is the binding design/content
standard for all future work on these decks - read it before any revision, don't work
from memory or a paraphrase of it.

## 1. Objective

Create a professional, graduate-level engineering PowerPoint presentation using the
provided project documentation and UNC Charlotte (UNCC) PowerPoint template files.

The presentation shall follow Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE)
technical writing, citation, and engineering presentation conventions. The final
presentation should resemble an academic engineering research presentation rather than
a generic AI-generated slideshow.

## 2. PowerPoint Template Requirements

- Use the provided UNCC PowerPoint template files as the foundation for the entire
  presentation.
- Use the provided title slide template for the opening slide.
- Use the provided content slide template for all subsequent slides.
- Preserve the original university branding, logos, backgrounds, color schemes, and
  footer formatting.
- Do not introduce additional themes or redesign the provided templates.
- Maintain consistent content positioning, margins, alignment, and spacing throughout
  the presentation.

## 3. Presentation Structure

Organize the presentation in a logical sequence that clearly communicates the
engineering project from introduction through conclusion.

The presentation shall include, at a minimum:

1. **Title Slide** - Project title; Author name; Course name and number; University;
   Presentation date
2. **Agenda Slide** - Major presentation sections, in the order they are presented
3. **Introduction and Background** - Engineering context; Problem statement; Project
   motivation; Research objectives
4. **Project Overview** - Proposed engineering solution; Project scope; System
   overview; Design requirements and constraints
5. **Engineering Methodology** - Technical approach; System design and architecture;
   Governing equations; Modeling and simulation; Experimental setup; Testing
   methodology
6. **Results and Analysis** - Experimental and simulation results; Engineering
   calculations; Graphs, figures, and tables; Performance comparisons; Interpretation
   of results
7. **Discussion** - Engineering significance; Observed trends; Measurement
   uncertainties; Technical limitations; Sources of error
8. **Conclusion** - Summary of major findings; Achievement of project objectives;
   Engineering feasibility; Final conclusions
9. **Future Work and Recommendations** - Potential design improvements; Additional
   testing recommendations; Opportunities for further research
10. **Questions Slide**
11. **References Slide(s)**

Combine or expand technical sections as necessary based on the project scope. Do not
create unnecessary slides solely to satisfy the suggested structure.

## 4. Font and Typography Requirements

Maintain consistent typography throughout the entire presentation. Use the font family
established by the provided UNCC templates. Unless otherwise required by the
templates, use the following recommended font sizes:

| Text Element | Recommended Font Size |
|---|---:|
| Presentation title | 36-44 pt |
| Slide titles | 28-32 pt |
| Section headings | 22-26 pt |
| Primary body text | 20-24 pt |
| Secondary body text | 18-20 pt |
| Table content | 14-18 pt |
| Figure and table captions | 12-14 pt |
| Citations | 12-14 pt |
| Reference entries | 14-16 pt |

- Use a consistent font size for equivalent content types across all slides.
- Do not arbitrarily resize text to fit overcrowded slides.
- Divide overcrowded content into additional slides when necessary.
- Maintain consistent capitalization, indentation, bullet formatting, and line
  spacing.
- Avoid excessive bolding, italicization, and underlining.
- Ensure all content remains readable when projected in a classroom or conference
  environment.

## 5. Visual Design and Formatting

The presentation shall use a clean, traditional, professional engineering
presentation style.

Avoid common AI-generated design characteristics, including:

- Excessive rounded rectangles and bubble-style formatting.
- Unnecessary cards, tiles, and floating text boxes.
- Decorative icons and graphics.
- Excessive colored backgrounds.
- Pill-shaped labels and headings.
- Artificial infographic-style layouts.
- Gradients, shadows, and unnecessary visual effects.
- Excessive borders and content containers.
- Unnecessary animations and transitions.
- Marketing-style language and layouts.

Prioritize the following instead:

- Traditional academic slide layouts.
- Clean, aligned text and technical figures.
- Professional engineering diagrams.
- Properly formatted equations.
- Structured tables and graphs.
- Consistent whitespace.
- Simple visual hierarchy.
- Purposeful use of the UNCC template colors.

Every visual element should serve a technical or informational purpose.

## 6. IEEE Citation and Reference Requirements

- Use numerical in-text citations in square brackets, such as [1], [2], and [3].
- Number references in the order of their first appearance.
- Reuse the original reference number whenever the same source is cited again.
- Place citations immediately after the applicable technical statement, equation,
  figure, table, or referenced information.
- Provide citations for externally sourced technical specifications, datasheets,
  standards, equations, figures, and research findings.
- Include a complete IEEE-formatted reference list on the final reference slide(s).
- Ensure every in-text citation has a corresponding reference entry.
- Ensure every reference entry is cited within the presentation.
- Do not fabricate references, authors, publications, Digital Object Identifiers
  (DOIs), or technical sources.
- When a supporting IEEE paper is provided, maintain citation consistency with that
  paper while ensuring the presentation's reference numbering follows the order of
  first appearance.

## 7. Acronym and Technical Terminology Requirements

All acronyms and abbreviations shall be spelled out upon their first occurrence
within the presentation, followed by the acronym in parentheses. Capitalize the
principal words of the expanded technical term for consistency.

Examples: Variable Frequency Drive (VFD); Instrumentation and Control (I&C);
Universal Asynchronous Receiver-Transmitter (UART); Root Mean Square (RMS); Pulse
Width Modulation (PWM).

After the initial definition, use only the acronym throughout the remainder of the
presentation. Maintain consistent engineering terminology, variable names, symbols,
units, and capitalization across all slides.

## 8. Engineering Equations and Calculations

- Use native, editable PowerPoint equation objects for mathematical equations.
- Do not insert equations as screenshots or improperly formatted plain text.
- Maintain consistent variable names with the provided project documentation.
- Define variables and their corresponding units when introduced.
- Use proper subscripts, superscripts, Greek symbols, and mathematical notation.
- Follow International System of Units (SI) conventions where applicable.
- Maintain consistent significant figures and numerical precision.
- Present sufficient calculations to communicate the engineering methodology without
  overcrowding slides.
- Do not introduce unsupported assumptions, equations, or numerical values.

## 9. Figures, Tables, and Graphs

- Number figures sequentially using IEEE-style labels, such as **Fig. 1.** and
  **Fig. 2.**
- Place figure captions below figures.
- Number tables using Roman numerals, such as **TABLE I** and **TABLE II**.
- Place table titles above tables.
- Include appropriate axis labels, units, legends, and captions.
- Maintain consistent numerical precision across comparable datasets.
- Ensure figures and graphs are sufficiently large to be readable during a
  presentation.
- Preserve the accuracy and integrity of all original data.
- Do not stretch, distort, or unnecessarily crop technical images.
- Use editable tables and charts whenever practical.
- Cite externally sourced figures, tables, and graphs.
- Do not create decorative graphics that provide no engineering value.

## 10. Technical Content and Accuracy

- Use the provided documentation as the primary source of technical information.
- Do not invent experimental results, calculations, equipment specifications, or
  conclusions.
- Do not introduce assumptions without explicitly identifying and justifying them.
- Maintain consistency between the presentation and the associated technical paper or
  report.
- Clearly distinguish measured, calculated, simulated, and theoretical results.
- Preserve documented technical limitations and uncertainties.
- Do not exaggerate project outcomes or engineering feasibility.
- Flag missing information, technical discrepancies, or unsupported claims for review
  rather than silently correcting or inventing information.

## 11. Writing Style and Presentation Flow

- Use concise, technically accurate, professional engineering language.
- Avoid unnecessary filler, repetitive statements, and generic AI-generated wording.
- Maintain a logical progression between slides and technical sections.
- Ensure each slide has a clear purpose and primary takeaway.
- Use concise bullet points rather than large paragraphs whenever practical.
- Avoid transferring entire paragraphs directly from the technical paper.
- Provide sufficient technical depth appropriate for a graduate-level engineering
  audience.
- Ensure conclusions are directly supported by the presented results.
- Balance technical completeness with presentation readability.

## 12. Final Quality Assurance Review

Before delivering the completed PowerPoint presentation, perform a comprehensive
audit verifying: correct use of the provided UNCC templates; consistent font
families and font sizes; consistent slide titles, margins, spacing, and alignment;
logical presentation structure and flow; agreement with the supporting technical
documentation; technical and mathematical accuracy; proper acronym definitions and
subsequent usage; correct IEEE citation and reference formatting; correct figure and
table numbering; consistent engineering terminology and units; absence of
overlapping, clipped, or misaligned content; absence of unnecessary AI-style visual
formatting; readability of equations, figures, tables, and graphs; completeness of
the title, agenda, conclusion, questions, and reference slides; correct spelling,
grammar, and punctuation.

## 13. Final Deliverables

- A fully editable Microsoft PowerPoint (.pptx) presentation.
- All slides formatted using the provided UNCC templates.
- Native editable text, equations, tables, and charts wherever practical.
- A brief summary of any technical discrepancies, missing information, assumptions,
  or unresolved formatting issues identified during development.

The completed presentation shall be suitable for a graduate-level engineering project
presentation, academic research presentation, thesis defense, or IEEE-style technical
conference presentation.

**Prioritize technical accuracy, readability, consistency, and professional academic
presentation standards over decorative visual design.**
